Is shockwave therapy the same as ESWT?

A: Yes. ESWT stands for extracorporeal shockwave therapy, the clinical name for this treatment; extracorporeal simply means the energy is generated outside the body and directed inward. There are two types. Focused ESWT (fESWT) converges acoustic energy at a set depth, concentrating it on the tissue being treated. Radial shockwave spreads its energy outward from the applicator head, so it stays closer to the surface. Both our Greeley and Denver clinics use focused ESWT. Because both types are commonly advertised simply as shockwave therapy, it is worth asking any provider which one they use.

Does insurance cover shockwave therapy?

A: Most commercial insurance plans do not cover shockwave therapy. Coverage and self-pay pricing can vary, so please contact our Greeley or Denver office for current details.

How many sessions will I need?

A: Most patients complete a program of 3-6 sessions, with initial improvement often noticeable after the first 2-3. Complex or long-standing injuries may benefit from a more extended treatment program. Your provider will evaluate your condition at your first visit and give you a realistic plan.

How soon will I notice results?

A: Many patients notice initial improvement after the first 2-3 sessions, with fuller results typically developing over the full 3-6 session program. The healing response continues for weeks after your program ends, meaning results often keep improving even after treatment is complete. Your provider tracks your progress throughout so you can see real change, not just feel it.

What does a shockwave session feel like?

A: Most patients describe it as a deep pressure or vibration, similar to a firm deep tissue massage. Sensitive areas may feel mildly uncomfortable, but it is generally well-tolerated. Your provider adjusts intensity based on your feedback throughout the session.

Is there downtime after treatment?

A: Most patients continue normal daily activities following treatment. Some mild soreness around the treated area is common and typically resolves within 24-48 hours. Your provider will give specific guidance based on your condition and the area being treated.
