Neuromuscular Massage Therapy in Greeley & Denver, CO

When pain keeps coming back to the same spot, the problem usually isn't where you feel it. Neuromuscular therapy finds the trigger points actually causing it and releases them — then addresses the posture and movement patterns that keep putting them back.

What Neuromuscular Therapy Actually Does

A trigger point is a tight, irritable band within a muscle that refers pain somewhere else. That's why a knot between your shoulder blades can produce a headache, and why tension in the glute can send pain down the leg in a pattern that looks like sciatica. Treating only where it hurts tends to give you a few hours of relief before it returns.

Neuromuscular therapy works the other direction. Your therapist palpates the muscle to locate the actual trigger point, applies sustained pressure until it releases, then works the surrounding tissue to restore normal length and circulation. Sessions typically address:

  • Trigger points and chronic muscle knots
  • Referred pain patterns — headaches, sciatic-type leg pain, arm and hand symptoms
  • Nerve compression from surrounding soft tissue
  • Postural strain from desk work, driving, or repetitive lifting
  • Ischemia — tissue that isn't getting enough blood flow to recover on its own
  • Scar tissue and restricted movement following an injury

Conditions We Treat This Way

Neuromuscular therapy is most useful for pain that is persistent, recurring, or clearly tied to a muscular pattern:

  • Chronic neck and upper-back pain
  • Tension headaches originating in the neck and shoulders
  • Low back pain and sciatic-type symptoms
  • Whiplash and soft-tissue injury after a car accident
  • Repetitive-strain and overuse injuries from work or training
  • Shoulder, hip, and jaw pain with a muscular component
  • Fibromyalgia and widespread chronic pain, as part of a broader plan

What Your First Session Looks Like

We start by asking where the pain is, what makes it worse, and how long it's been going on — then we assess how you move and where the tissue is actually restricted. Those two things often point to different places, and that gap is usually the useful information.

Treatment is firm and specific rather than gentle and general. Pressure on an active trigger point is tender, and you may briefly feel the referred pain reproduce before it lets go. It should stay within a range you can breathe through — tell your therapist if it doesn't, because working past that point makes the muscle guard and defeats the purpose. Mild soreness for a day or two afterward is normal.

You'll leave with something to do between visits. Releasing a trigger point without changing what created it just buys time, so most plans pair hands-on work with corrective movement, and often with physical therapy or chiropractic care when the underlying issue is structural.

AFTER A CAR ACCIDENT OR
WORKPLACE INJURY

Soft-tissue injury is the part of a crash that imaging often misses. You can have a clean X-ray and still be unable to turn your head, because the damage is in muscle and fascia rather than bone. Neuromuscular therapy is well suited to that, and it's a routine part of how we treat auto injuries and work comp claims.

Neuromuscular work is frequently covered under auto injury (MedPay) and workers' compensation claims, and some health plans cover it as part of a documented treatment plan. Coverage depends on your specific policy and claim — call us before you book and we'll check your benefits and tell you straight what's covered.

COMMON QUESTIONS

How is this different from a regular massage?

A relaxation massage works broadly across the whole body. Neuromuscular therapy is targeted: the therapist works to find the specific trigger points responsible for your pain and releases them individually. It's a clinical treatment aimed at a problem, not an hour of general relaxation — though most people find it relaxing anyway once the tissue lets go.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on how long the pattern has been present and what's driving it. A recent strain may resolve in a few visits; long-standing postural pain generally takes longer and responds better when combined with rehab. You'll get a realistic estimate after the first evaluation, not an open-ended package.
